原文摘要 · Abstract
For decades, non-player characters (NPCs) have been driven by rule-based systems whose personalities and interactions are scripted before release. Large language models (LLMs) promise NPCs that converse openly, adapt to new situations, and behave in ways developers did not fully author, prompting a wave of research and commercial experimentation. Yet the field lacks a unified picture of what has been built, how well it works, and at what cost. We address this gap with a systematic mapping study of 377 papers from the academic literature, yielding 42 papers analysed against a main research question and ten sub-questions across four thematic axes. We provide the first quantitative characterisation of component adoption, latency, safety coverage, and reproducibility: short-term memory and world-state grounding are near-universal (60% and 69%), whereas long-term memory (31%), reflection (38%), and retrieval-augmented generation (14%) remain rare, and response times span 2.6 to 15 seconds. Finally, we identify six structural barriers to commercial adoption and offer evidence-based recommendations for developers and researchers.

原文摘要 · Abstract
Interactive narratives let users shape a story while an Experience Manager (EM) keeps it coherent, balancing player freedom against authorial control. Large Language Models now enable a new generation of hybrid EMs, which combine run-time generation from free-text input with symbolic narrative control. However, their heterogeneity makes them hard to compare: A manager's behaviour is generally tied to the unique and specific scenario in which it operates, and much of its decision-making takes place outside of any explicit and verifiable structure. We address this with an LLM-based narrative platform whose managers are interchangeable modules operating over a shared symbolic world model of facts, operations, and goals. Because every applied operation carries explicit preconditions and effects, each session can be analysed as a plan through a common state-transition representation and evaluated, independently of how the manager works internally, with shared indicators of four properties: player agency, EM controllability, author intent, and robustness. We implement three contrasting managers, based respectively on open generation, Monte Carlo repair, and autonomous characters, and compare them through functional analysis on an interactive Little Red Riding Hood scenario. The results show that no manager dominates on every property, giving authors and EM designers a concrete basis to select a manager according to their priorities.

原文摘要 · Abstract
Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) experiences are difficult to share with the public because they are essentially processes that rely on physical interactions with embodied, tacit, and situated phenomena in specific cultural contexts. We consume non-interactive media such as videos and books to learn about culinary ICH experiences, but they do not allow us to grasp actual interactive procedures that embody the cultural knowledge. Compared against watching a video in VR providing the same information in a between-subjects study (N=40), Hakka Kitchen led to increased sensory, imaginative engagement, positive affect, and willingness to transmit awareness for the culinary ICH. Heritage recognition is mode-dependent: the cognitive load of enactment suppressed players’ uptake of concurrent cultural narration; however, it generated somatic and memory anchors that fostered a deeper, implicit kinesthetic form of empathy for the artisan’s labor and cultural background. Our work shows how representing interactive procedures instead of static content may empower cultural awareness in a virtual form of ICH practice.

原文摘要 · Abstract
Understanding how diverse audiences engage with narratives and content is central to exhibition design, yet designers often rely on intuition. Existing experience evaluation methods are typically retrospective, costly, and offer limited access to visitors’ internal states, hindering early-stage iterative refinement. Rather than relying only on post-implementation evaluation with real visitors, we explore LLM-driven persona simulation as a reference for early-stage design. SiMUSation models diverse visitor personas and simulates their exhibition experiences through a dual-layer representation that couples observable behaviors, such as movement and gaze, with corresponding internal responses, such as confusion and narrative engagement. Designers can steer simulations, inspect feedback from simulated visits, and iteratively revise layouts, content, and narrative flow to further examine how changes reshape visitor experience. We implemented a prototype and evaluated it through a user study (N=12), showing that SiMUSation provides insights for reflection and refinement in early-stage exhibition design. Our findings further highlight the potential of persona-driven simulation to support audience-informed evaluation and iterative decision-making across design tasks.

原文摘要 · Abstract
Metadata-conditioned generation is often framed as a data-to-text task in which additional input fields should improve grounding. Museum description generation challenges this assumption because catalogue records are heterogeneous, partially visual, and unevenly documented across geographic regions. We study how metadata composition shapes the generation of artefact descriptions through a two-phase framework. Phase 1 analyses natural metadata variation in a large museum collection, where field count, field type, image availability, and Place co-vary. Phase 2 introduces controlled field probes that hold object identity fixed while varying metadata quantity, field type, and image access. Our results show that metadata quantity alone does not explain generation behaviour. Field type determines whether models ground, omit, or infer information, while images can both support visual grounding and increase unsupported interpretation. More broadly, our findings show that metadata-conditioned data-to-text evaluation should assess field- and claim-level support, rather than relying only on aggregate input coverage or reference similarity.

原文摘要 · Abstract
Paper stationery processing technique is a national-level intangible cultural heritage (ICH) that embodies artisanal value and traditional literati aesthetics, yet it faces structural challenges including transmission discontinuity, fragile material carriers, dispersed craft resources and insufficient endogenous viability. Generative artificial intelligence (AI) opens a new technological pathway for the digital safeguarding of living craft ICH, but existing research has largely focused on image restoration and digital exhibition of tangible heritage, while micro-level case evidence and ethical boundary analysis for living craft ICH remain underdeveloped. Taking the national ICH "Paper Stationery Processing Technique" as its object, this study adopts a single-case study method and literature analysis to examine four application dimensions: rescue preservation, resource governance, assisted transmission and dissemination activation. It focuses on the "AIGC pattern generation–3D-printed mold–manual application" composite chain, assessing both its value for expanding paper stationery processes and its applicable boundaries. The study identifies structural risks of technological intervention: erosion of authenticity, ambiguous copyright attribution, weakening of craft subjectivity and the digital divide. Findings indicate that generative AI can significantly improve the efficiency of ICH archival construction and broaden cultural dissemination; 3D-printed molds can serve as a new craft intermediary for pattern processing. However, the legitimate application boundary is confined to the resource infrastructure layer and the tool-assistance layer, and must not intrude upon the epistemological layer of craft. Algorithms and digital molds can undertake externalizable technical tasks such as pattern conception, material retrieval and model formation, but cannot replace the embodied perception, situated judgment and manual practice that artisans develop through material engagement. Grounded in the principle of "bearer-led, technology-assisted", this paper proposes four governance strategies—tiered labeling, authorized training, practice priority and inclusive tools—and distils generalizable implications for the digital safeguarding of comparable living craft skills.

原文摘要 · Abstract
As generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) becomes increasingly embedded in digital products, understanding how users experience GenAI-enabled products has become an important research issue. Prior research on GenAI products has mainly examined relatively static AI-generated outputs, with particular attention to disclosure, algorithmic source, authenticity, and perceived value. However, this literature offers limited insight into digital games, where GenAI may be incorporated not only into pre-generated components such as assets, text, and audio, but also into runtime systems such as adaptive dialogue, characters, and gameplay content. To address this gap, this study develops a Needs-(Dis)Affordances-Features framework for understanding how GenAI-enabled technical features shape players’ psychological need satisfaction and frustration in digital games. Drawing on the needs-affordances-features perspective, disaffordance, and self-determination theory, we conduct a grounded theory analysis of 3,096 reviews from 810 digital games on Steam identified through platform statements indicating GenAI involvement. The findings suggest that player responses are shaped less by AI-related labels or source cues than by the specific technical features through which GenAI is incorporated into the game. GenAI-enabled features such as generated assets, dialogue, and adaptive systems give rise to affordances and disaffordances that support or frustrate autonomy, competence, and relatedness. This study extends research on GenAI products to digital games by showing how feature-level GenAI integration, rather than AI origin alone, shapes player experience.

原文摘要 · Abstract
This systematic review examines how Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) may influence local knowledge in tourism, focusing on algorithmic mediation, epistemic authority, and representational justice. Synthesizing 35 articles across four literature streams published primarily between 2023 and 2026, covering AI technologies, local knowledge systems, computational mediation, and cultural representation, the paper examines how conversational travel agents and large language models may influence destination storytelling. The synthesis indicates that GenAI can function as an active intermediary rather than simply as a neutral information channel. Eleven operational transformations are identified, including selection, translation, de-contextualization, recombination, and redistribution, through which local knowledge may be altered as it moves into AI-mediated tourism environments. The review suggests that these processes may reproduce existing differences in tourism knowledge visibility, particularly where AI systems rely heavily on digitally available and commercially prominent information while local, oral, or less digitally represented knowledge remains comparatively less visible. The literature also identifies concerns regarding AI-generated inaccuracies, including historical hallucinations and cultural simplification, and suggests that the fluency of AI-generated content may contribute to perceptions of authority among users. From a knowledge governance perspective, the synthesis raises concerns about the implications of extracting and processing traditional and Indigenous knowledge without adequate community participation, consent, attribution, or benefit-sharing, particularly in relation to Indigenous Data Sovereignty. Rather than concluding that GenAI inevitably displaces traditional knowledge authorities, the review suggests that it may introduce an additional layer of influence through which tourists encounter and evaluate destination knowledge. To address these concerns, the paper proposes a six-pillar Responsible AI Governance Framework encompassing cultural sensitivity, transparency, inclusivity, community-informed co-design, platform accountability, and respect for local ownership. The review concludes that responsible integration of GenAI in tourism requires greater attention to human oversight, community participation, knowledge governance, and the rights of local knowledge holders.
