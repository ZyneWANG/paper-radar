中文导读
论文区分了故事的事件顺序和讲述顺序，构建了83部已出版作品的基准，标注事件双序、因果依赖、角色知识和悬念设置，测试19个模型能否生成非线性叙事。结果发现模型普遍倾向于按时间顺序平铺直叙，即使指定结构也只有约一半能达到人类水平。
原文摘要 · Abstract
Every story has two orders: the order in which its events happen and the order in which they are told. A story is nonlinear when the two differ. We introduce a benchmark of 83 published works that measures whether language models can tell nonlinear stories. For each work, we annotate one list of events in both orders, together with a story graph of causal dependencies, character knowledge, and the questions the telling raises and resolves. Given events in chronological order, all 19 models tell them, on average, more chronologically than the published works do; asked for a specific structure, they land within the human range about half the time. We then compare a model's stories of the same events under different conditions, using both an automated judge and 71 human readers. Both prefer the chronological story a model writes by default to the nonlinear one it writes on request, which readers find harder to follow in 81% of decisive judgments. Readers also prefer stories written from the story graph to stories constrained to the published telling order (72% of decisive judgments), a difference the judge does not detect. Our results suggest that following an instruction to reorder a story is not yet the same as telling it well out of order.

原文摘要 · Abstract
This article presents a design experiment investigating whether and how cultural heritage values may emerge in the development of a serious game created through generative AI. Centered on Edgar à GoGo—a game based on Edgar Varèse’s Poème Électronique—the study explores whether meaningful aspects of the heritage item can take shape in the game’s components, even in the absence of explicitly formalized values. The game concept was generated using ChatGPT within the Co.Lab design framework, followed by a prototyping phase in which the authors, acting as developers, posed implementation-driven clarification questions. The resulting prototype was evaluated by experts familiar with the original work, whose feedback highlighted both latent value alignments and critical omissions. While some values weakly emerged through the AI’s design logic, others were absent, prompting reflective discussion. The study proposes a hybrid, iterative approach to value-sensitive design in cultural heritage games, positioning expert evaluation as a crucial layer for value articulation and refinement.This work has been supported by the project PNRR - CHANGES (Cultural Heritage Active Innovation for Next-Gen Sustainable Society), Grant Agreement PE00000020. It is also part of the research within IRACF – INTERNATIONAL ROUTES: ARTS CREATING FUTURE (INTAFAM_00055).

原文摘要 · Abstract

原文摘要 · Abstract

We present ILM, an interactive educational platform for Stories of the Prophets that combines Arabic natural language processing, structured knowledge representation, and retrieval-based question generation. 原文摘要 · Abstract
This study integrates Flow Theory and Embodied Cognition to examine the transmission of Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH), addressing the psychological distance between contemporary audiences and traditional cultural practices. A randomized experiment ( N = 150) compared text-image, audiovisual, and interactive instrumental-performance conditions. The interactive condition produced the highest Flow Experience and Experience Satisfaction, followed by the audiovisual and text-image conditions; Welch tests and Games-Howell comparisons confirmed this ordered pattern under unequal variances. The Audiovisual-versus-Interactive contrast retained a significant ordinary indirect effect. These results are consistent with Flow Experience functioning as a condition-dependent proximal psychological pathway rather than a uniform or causally identified mechanism. These findings inform the design of brief heritage experiences. Their implications for sustained participation require longitudinal and field-based evaluation.
